For people who run many Claude Code sessions

The conductor for your Claude sessions.

Thalamus is a small Windows tray app. It sees every Claude Code session you run, keeps your shells alive, tells you what to work on next, files your ideas into the right project, and pings your phone only when you are away.

The problem

Five sessions. Five windows. One has been waiting for 40 minutes.

Claude Code works best when you run several sessions side by side: one per project, sometimes more. But the terminals pile up, a session quietly finishes and waits for an answer, and you only notice when you happen to switch to it. Ideas land in a notes file and never reach the project. And somewhere in the background your 5-hour limit is running down.

Without Thalamus
Click through every terminal to see which one needs you. Keep a to-do list by hand. Guess how much of the limit is left. Lose a long-running shell because a window closed.
With Thalamus
One board shows every session by project, with a state you read at a glance. A short ranked list says what to do next. Ideas you drop in are filed to their project. Shells survive the window, your limits sit in the title bar, and your phone buzzes only when you are away and something needs you.

Everything that runs, on one screen.

Next up: three short lists

Every 30 minutes Claude looks at the sessions waiting for you, the inbox and the backlog, and ranks what to do next in three columns: Work, Private and General. They have their own tab right of the Cockpit (Ctrl+6), which counts what is new since you last looked.

  • Start or Discard. Start hands the item to its project's session, or opens one.
  • It learns. What you start and what you skip shapes the next ranking.
  • The backlog keeps itself. When a session clearly works on a card, the card moves to Doing, and to Done when it is finished. Only when it is sure, and you can move it back.

Inbox: drop it and forget it

Paste or drop anything into Thalamus: text, a screenshot, a file, with a comment if you like. Claude files clear items straight into the right Engram project. Unclear ones wait with a suggestion, and Wrong project corrects a filing and teaches it for next time.

Daydream

Ideas you had not had yet.

Named after the brain's default mode network, which comes up with ideas while you are busy with something else. Every few hours Claude looks at what is going on across your projects: the sessions, the inbox, the backlog and what your memory noted recently. Then it proposes a few new ideas: a next feature, a link between two projects, a risk worth heading off.

  • Greenlight or Discard. Greenlight puts an idea on a project's backlog, or opens a new project for it. Discard teaches Daydream not to propose it again.
  • Never in your way. Ideas wait in their own list, not in the inbox. A run is skipped when nothing changed, or when you were away the whole time.
  • Your rhythm. Every 2 hours to start, from 30 minutes to once a day, with the model you choose. /ideas and /daydream work from your phone too.

Clarify

Turns terse notes like "invoice gaps??" into a clear title, why and done-when, and flags cards that look done or duplicate another.

Suggest from Engram

Claude reads your projects' memory and proposes cards. You tick the ones worth keeping; nothing is added without you.

Start from a card

Open a card for its details, then Start: the work goes to its project's Claude session, or a new one opens.

Telegram

Your phone, only when it matters.

Pair your own Telegram bot with your account, and Thalamus can reach you when you step away. It is quiet by design: it only pings when you are away from the laptop, idle or locked.

  • You choose how loud. Off, Approvals only, Important or Everything. A cap on messages per hour, and quiet hours that end with a single summary.
  • Approvals at once, answers batched. Approval requests arrive straight away. Waiting answers collect in one silent message that updates in place.
  • Reply to answer. Reply to a ping and your text goes to that session as its next instruction.
  • Capture on the go. Send an idea, a photo or a file: Thalamus sorts it and replies where it went, with a Wrong project button, or that it waits in the inbox. /next shows what is up, with Go and Skip; /ideas brings Daydream's proposals.
  • Look in and act. /board shows everything that runs, /tail the last lines of a tab, /inbox what waits to be filed. /send gives a project's session an instruction; /sh queues a PowerShell command that waits for your approval.
  • Approvals with a code. Shell commands and Claude permission prompts are approved with a code from your authenticator app, set up once by scanning a QR code. A code approves exactly the prompt you saw, never a newer one.

What it does today

Built for a full day of sessions.

Sees every session

Every Claude Code session reports in, wherever you start it. Sessions started elsewhere show as "outside" and move into a tab with one click. Ended ones wait under Recent, ready to resume.

States at a glance

Bars while a session works, an amber ! for a new answer, a grey one once you have seen it, a ? for an approval, a chip when commands run in the background. The Cockpit tab counts what needs you.

Shells that survive

Terminals live in a separate background process. Close the window, restart it, update the app: your shells and Claude sessions keep running.

Tabs, side by side

Every terminal is a tab. On a wide screen, drag tabs between two groups and watch two sessions at once. The keys you know from Windows Terminal: selection, copy and paste, find, zoom.

Drop files, click links

Drop files on a terminal and that session gets their paths. Ctrl+click opens a link, and Ctrl+click on a suggested ! command puts it into Claude's prompt.

Pick and switch the model

Choose the model when you start a session, with a guide to its speed and how much of your limits it uses. Switch it later with a click on the board.

Limits, tokens and context

Your 5-hour and weekly limits as two batteries in the title bar. Token history per day, week or month by project and model. Each session shows its recent activity like a task monitor and how full its context window is, and its panel charts token use and context growth over time, with every compaction marked.

Commands wait for you

Commands proposed for a PowerShell run only after you approve them. Tasks for a project go to its session, or open one, and never to two sessions at once.

Settings your way

Dark, light or system theme, size per screen, terminal font, side-by-side terminals and your own shortcuts: Ctrl+N Claude session, Ctrl+P PowerShell, Ctrl+1–6 views, Ctrl+, settings.

How it works

Small pieces, all on your laptop.

Thalamus uses what Claude Code already offers: hooks and the status line. No extension, no proxy in front of the API, no account.

Claude Code sessions

In any terminal. Hooks report start, prompts, tool use, waiting and end. The status line reports limits, model and context fill.

→ 127.0.0.1 →

Thalamus

Tray app with the board, Next up, inbox, Daydream ideas, backlog, usage, the terminals and the optional Telegram bot. Installs signed updates by itself.

⇄ pipe ⇄

Shell host

A background process that owns your terminals, so they outlive the window and every update.

Private by design

Your laptop does the work.

Thalamus has no server and no telemetry. It runs on your own machine and only reads what Claude Code writes there. The only messages that leave it are the Telegram pings you switch on.

  • Local only. The server listens on 127.0.0.1, needs a per-install token and refuses anything a web page could send.
  • Your own Claude login. Ranking, filing and clarifying run as Claude calls on your own plan. No other AI service is involved.
  • Secrets stay put. Tokens live in one local file, never in logs or messages; your Telegram token and approval secret are encrypted with Windows' own data protection. Text sent to Telegram is redacted first.
  • Nothing runs by itself. Shell commands always wait for your approval, and text sent to a Claude session can never turn into one. Everything Thalamus does is kept in an audit log you can read in Settings.
  • Report-only hooks. The token Claude's hooks use can report events, nothing else. It cannot start, read or control a shell.
  • Your settings, your call. Hooks are added to your Claude settings only after you see the exact change and confirm it. A backup is made first.

Works well with Engram

Thalamus runs your sessions. Engram remembers them.

Engram is the memory your AI keeps: every session writes what it did and decided into a local memory, and the next session starts from there instead of from zero. Thalamus and Engram sit side by side in the tray: one shows what is running right now, the other what was learned along the way.

Both organise work by project and keep everything on your laptop. The inbox files your ideas straight into Engram projects, and the backlog is built from what Engram knows is still open.

A good pair
Drop an idea into Thalamus and it lands in the right Engram project. Start it from Next up, and the session picks up its project's memory. When it finishes, Thalamus tells you; Engram has already written down what happened.

Get Engram

Install

Up and running in two minutes.

  1. Download the installer

    Get Thalamus-Setup.exe from the latest release.

  2. Trust the signing certificate (once)

    Thalamus is signed with its own certificate, not one bought from a certificate authority. Windows has to be told to trust it once, otherwise the installer shows "Unknown publisher" and automatic updates are refused. Run this in PowerShell; it downloads the public certificate, checks its fingerprint and adds it for your user only (Windows asks you to confirm):

    $c = "$env:TEMP\thalamus-code-signing.cer"
Invoke-WebRequest https://martindroneharmony.github.io/thalamus-releases/thalamus-code-signing.cer -OutFile $c
if ((Get-PfxCertificate $c).Thumbprint -ne '3FD6F579455657834DBD444A55B9FC38F8CD8C49') { throw 'Fingerprint does not match, do not install.' }
Import-Certificate $c -CertStoreLocation Cert:\CurrentUser\Root | Out-Null
Import-Certificate $c -CertStoreLocation Cert:\CurrentUser\TrustedPublisher | Out-Null

    The certificate can only sign programs (nothing else, such as websites) and expires in October 2031. To remove it later, delete Thalamus Code Signing from both stores in certmgr.msc.

  3. Run it

    It installs for your user only, no admin rights needed. The publisher shows as Thalamus Code Signing. As a new program it may still meet "Windows protected your PC": choose More info, then Run anyway.

  4. Find it in the tray

    Thalamus starts with Windows and lives in the tray. Click the icon to open the cockpit; the gear (Ctrl+,) opens the settings.

  5. Let your sessions report

    Click Set up the hooks…. Thalamus shows exactly what it adds to your Claude settings and makes a backup first.

  6. Start Claude anywhere

    Run claude in any terminal, or press Ctrl+N in Thalamus and pick a project. The session appears on the board.

  7. Optional: your phone

    In Settings, under Telegram, pair your own bot and scan the QR code with your authenticator app. Pick how much you want to hear.

  8. Optional: limits and context fill

    If you use a Claude status line script in PowerShell, add this so it also passes the limits and each session's context fill to Thalamus ($raw is the JSON your script read from standard input):

    try { $t = Get-Content "$env:LOCALAPPDATA\Thalamus\report.json" -Raw -ErrorAction Stop | ConvertFrom-Json
      Invoke-RestMethod -Uri $t.url -Method Post -Headers @{ Authorization = "Bearer $($t.token)" } `
        -ContentType 'application/json' -Body $raw -TimeoutSec 1 | Out-Null } catch {}
