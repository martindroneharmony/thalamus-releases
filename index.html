The problem

Five sessions. Five windows. One has been waiting for 40 minutes.

Claude Code works best when you run several sessions side by side: one per project, sometimes more. But the terminals pile up, a session quietly finishes and waits for an answer, and you only notice when you happen to switch to it. Ideas land in a notes file and never reach the project. And somewhere in the background your 5-hour limit is running down.

Without Thalamus
Click through every terminal to see which one needs you. Keep a to-do list by hand. Guess how much of the limit is left. Lose a long-running shell because a window closed.
With Thalamus
One list shows every session by project, with a state you read at a glance. A short ranked list says what to do next. Ideas you drop in are filed to their project. Shells survive the window, your limits sit in the title bar, and your phone buzzes only when you are away and something needs you.

The cockpit

Everything that runs, on one screen.

Every session in one list on top, your terminals as tabs below, and the Board one tab over. On a wide screen the terminals split into two groups side by side.

In the cockpit

Sees every session

Every Claude Code session reports in, wherever you start it, named after the folder it runs in. Sessions started elsewhere show as "outside" and move into a tab with one click. Ended ones wait under Recent, ready to resume; sessions Thalamus started stay in the History with the item they worked on.

Limits, tokens and context

Your 5-hour and weekly limits as two batteries in the title bar, with when the 5-hour limit runs out at this pace. Token history by day, project and model. Each session shows its recent token use and how full its context is, with Compact one click away.

Pick and switch the model

Choose the model when you start a session, with a guide to its speed and what it takes of your limits. Switch it later with a click on its row. Fast mode for Opus is one switch per session and shows only what Claude confirmed.

Shells that survive

Terminals live in a separate background process. Close the window, restart it, update the app: your shells and Claude sessions keep running. When an update is ready, Thalamus asks whether to restart now or later.

Tabs, side by side

Every terminal is a tab. On a wide screen, drag tabs between two groups and watch two sessions at once. The keys you know from Windows Terminal: selection, copy and paste, find, zoom.

Drop files, click links

Drop files on a terminal and the session gets their paths. Ctrl+click opens a link. Right-click copies it whole, even when it wraps; a file link copies as a Windows path. Ctrl+click on a suggested ! command puts it into Claude's prompt.

Commands wait for you

Commands proposed for a PowerShell run only after you approve them. Tasks for a project go to its session, or open one once it is needed, and never to two sessions at once.

A session that forgets

The General button opens one Claude session in your projects folder that keeps nothing: no memory notes, nothing on the Board. It can still read your memory when you ask, and it comes back when it ends.

Keys and settings

Ctrl+K finds any item, project, session or action. Dark, light or system theme, size per screen, terminal font. Your own shortcuts: Ctrl+N Claude session, Ctrl+P PowerShell, Ctrl+1 to 3 for Cockpit, Board and Usage, Ctrl+, settings.

Board

From idea to done, in plain sight.

Every idea, bug and task moves through six stages: Seed, Shaping, Ready, Doing, Review, Done. Two moves are always yours: the Go that turns a shaped idea into work, and the Done that closes it. Each project has its own lane; lanes with work under way sit on top.

The lit tabs

One place for each kind of thing that waits, never two: a tab shows up with its count when something waits and is gone when nothing does.

  • Approvals, Decisions, Questions, Review, Due. Permission prompts and mail access in Approvals; Claude's choices in Decisions; questions from sessions and shaping, failing plans, briefs ready for your Go, stopped steps, captures to file and new ideas in Questions; finished work to check in Review, with its proof; deadlines in Due, soonest first. Done stays your move in the item.
  • A weekly review. On Monday a short guided review from the Board: what waits, this week's bets and a retro that goes into Engram.
  • Focus when you need it. A focus block holds every ping except approvals and tells you afterwards what came up.

New: write it, drop it, forget it

One box at the top of the Board takes everything: a sentence, a screenshot, a file, a long paste. Drop or paste anywhere and it lands there. Pick a project and it is a Seed at once: the list is your project folders, read from disk, so a new folder is there the next time you look; leave it to Thalamus and Claude files it into the right Engram project and puts it on the board as a Seed, with its kind and tags. Unclear ones wait in the same box with a suggestion, and Wrong project corrects a filing and teaches it for next time.

Capture

Capture anything

A new item takes a sentence, files, a pasted screenshot or a long paste. A long capture becomes a card with a short topic name; the whole text stays inside the item. Attachments stay with the item in its project folder, out of git.

Kinds and tags

Each item is a Bug, a Feature request, a Decision, Research or a Chore, with tags such as Slides, Testing or Publish. Thalamus sets them as it files, learns from your corrections, and never changes the ones you chose.

From any session

Claude sessions put an item on the Board with thalamus add, with its tags and due date, keep it current with thalamus edit, and hand finished work over to Review. A long hand-over is stored whole or refused, never cut, and the command says how much it stored. With thalamus list and thalamus show they first see what is already there, so nothing lands twice. Go, Done and every other move stay yours: the command refuses them.

Shape

It asks you first

"Ask me questions" on an idea and Claude asks the few questions it needs, here or on your phone, each with a recommended answer. Then it writes the brief: problem, why, outcome, done when.

Plan and steps

Your Go turns the brief into steps, each with its acceptance criteria. The plan is checked before it runs and revised once by itself; you are asked only if it still falls short. Start is one click and tells you if Claude did not take the instruction.

Dates and waits

Give an item a hard deadline or a target date: the Due tab lists what is overdue or due within a week, a folded lane still shows its hard dates, and one push a morning tells you how many are coming, plus any hard date nothing has moved on for a week. Mark what waits for a person or another item. Park an item until a day you choose, or drop it with a reason. Repeat chores monthly or yearly.

Run

Steps in parallel

Each step of a bigger item can run in its own Claude session, on its own git worktree and branch. Every step hands over its proof. Your Done merges the branches, only into a clean working copy, and stops on a conflict.

Run all steps

After your Go, Thalamus can run a plan's steps one after another, each test-first with its own proof. It stops at the first step that misses a criterion, and before any step with a link, a download or a destructive command, and asks you.

Limits

At most three items in Doing and a handful waiting for you, both adjustable, so work does not pile up faster than you can check it.

Check

Proof

A finished session hands over its proof: each done-when point with evidence, what it could not verify, and what to try yourself. Open the item in Review and its outcome sits on top; your Done moves it at once.

Review, then publish

Finished work arrives with its hand tests as a checklist. Slides, documents and designs get a second look against done-when before you see them. Publish runs only after your OK, and from your phone only after Face ID or your fingerprint.

Approvals, Decisions, Questions, Review, Due

Five tabs gather what waits for you: every permission prompt the moment it is raised, with its command and risk; every choice Claude offers, its option questions and plan approvals; every open question from sessions, shaping and plans, and what waits on the Board; finished work to check; and dates coming up. Answer right there and the row goes at once; Show all gives the whole command, file or plan behind a row. The tabs vanish when nothing waits.

Approve a session in one go

When one session asks again and again, Approve all gives it a level until it ends: file edits only, all but risky, or everything. The prompt waiting at that moment is approved with it, and covered prompts go through without asking (with ECC's GateGuard, so do its first-edit checks outside the project); deletes, pushes, installs, settings and key files still come to you below Everything, and Claude's questions always do. The session's row shows the level, and one click takes it back.

More about the Board: lanes, folders, automatic moves, mail

Lanes

Lanes start folded to their name and count and stay as you leave them; Show Doing opens every lane with work under way. Drag a card into another lane and it moves to that project, folder included. Done stays off the Board until you tick it. Done and Drop sit on the cards themselves, two clicks each. On a laptop nothing scrolls sideways.

Projects are folders

Start an item and it goes to the Claude session in that project's folder, with that project's instructions and memory. No session there yet? One opens. Any folder under your projects folder works, at any depth; there is no project list to maintain: the Project list of a new item is the folder tree itself, and each lane on the Board carries its folder's name.

The Board keeps itself

Start moves an item to Doing, and the review packet its session hands over moves it to Review. A card in Doing shows what its session is doing right now, with the same signs as the Cockpit. Done stays your call.

Mail only with your OK

A session that wants to read a mail asks first, with its reason. You see sender, date and subject and grant that thread, one search, or the mailbox. The grant is read-only, for that session, for at most an hour.

Daydream

Ideas you had not had yet.

Named after the brain's default mode network, which comes up with ideas while you are busy with something else. Every few hours Claude looks at what is going on across your projects: the sessions, your captures, the Board and what your memory noted recently. Then it proposes a few new ideas: a next feature, a link between two projects, a risk worth heading off.

  • Promote or Drop. Promote makes an idea a Seed on the board, or opens a new project for it. A dropped idea stays dropped for good: Daydream never proposes it again, not even reworded.
  • Few, and judged first. Daydream thinks up more candidates than it shows, takes its time over them, and a strict second look keeps only the best: at most a handful per run, and none when none is good enough.
  • Never in your way. Ideas wait in their own list, apart from your own items. A run is skipped when nothing changed, or when you were away the whole time.
  • Your rhythm. Every 2 hours to start, from 30 minutes to once a day, with the model you choose.

Your phone

The board in your pocket, Face ID on what matters.

Between meetings or on the train, open Thalamus on your phone: the board, what waits for you, and the decisions, from the Thalamus running on your laptop. It travels only through your own Tailscale network, never the open internet.

  • Paired once, revoked in a click. Scan a QR code from Settings › Phone. The phone keeps its own key; Revoke cuts it off at once, open page and all.
  • Face ID, not codes. After 15 minutes away, and before anything risky (approving a command, a Done that merges branches), Face ID or your fingerprint. It approves exactly what you see, the whole command, once.
  • The whole board, by touch. Every move the desktop card has, Park and Drop, edit an item, its kind and tags, steps and Run all steps, hand tests, who it waits for, and its timeline. One stage at a time, live, sized for a thumb on phones and tablets.
  • Approvals come to you. A command or a permission prompt that needs you pops up at once with the whole command: Approve with your fingerprint, Deny, or Later, or approve everything that session asks from now on, at the level you pick.
  • Capture and ask. An idea, a photo or a dictated note, with its kind and tags, to the New box or straight to a project. A quick question to a running session or the General session: watch it work step by step like a console, then copy or share the answer.
  • Notifications that say little. Only while you are away from the laptop, with levels, categories, quiet hours and Mute, set from the phone or from Settings. A notification says how many things wait, never what or for whom.

How it works

Small pieces, all on your laptop.

Thalamus uses what Claude Code already offers: hooks and the status line. No extension, no proxy in front of the API, no account.

Claude Code sessions

In any terminal. Hooks report start, prompts, tool use, waiting and end. The status line reports limits, model and context fill.

→ 127.0.0.1 →

Thalamus

Tray app with the cockpit of sessions, the Board (New, Daydream ideas, the items), the tabs of what waits for you, usage, the terminals and the optional phone page. Fetches signed updates and installs them when you say so.

⇄ pipe ⇄

Shell host

A background process that owns your terminals, so they outlive the window and every update.

Private by design

Your laptop does the work.

Thalamus has no cloud service and no telemetry. It runs on your own machine and reads only what Claude Code writes there. What leaves the laptop: Claude calls on your own plan, the update check at GitHub, and, if you switch them on, push notifications that say how many things wait and nothing more. Your phone reaches it only through your own Tailscale network.

  • Local only. Its local server listens on 127.0.0.1, needs a per-install token and refuses anything a web page could send.
  • Your own Claude login. Ranking, filing and clarifying run as Claude calls on your own plan. No other AI service is involved.
  • Secrets stay put. Tokens live in one local file and never appear in logs or notifications. The phone holds its own key; Thalamus keeps only a hash.
  • No shell command without you. Shell commands always wait for your approval, and text sent to a Claude session can never turn into one. Everything Thalamus does is kept in an audit log you can read in Settings.
  • Report-only hooks. The token Claude's hooks use can report events, nothing else. It cannot start, read or control a shell.
  • Your settings, your call. Hooks are added to your Claude settings only after you see the exact change and confirm it. A backup is made first.
  • Tool servers you name. A project's own tool servers start in Thalamus's sessions without the usual question only when you list them in Settings. The list starts empty.

Works well with Engram

Thalamus runs your sessions. Engram remembers them.

Engram is the memory your AI keeps: every session writes what it did and decided into a local memory, and the next session starts from there instead of from zero. Thalamus and Engram sit side by side in the tray: one shows what is running right now, the other what was learned along the way.

Both organise work by project and keep everything on your laptop. Thalamus files your captures into the projects Engram knows, and writes a record to Engram when an item is done. Its Memory tab searches your whole memory as you type and opens any memory whole, with its links; thalamus recall and thalamus search answer from the same index in a tenth of a second. As a trial, sessions in Thalamus tabs can also write their memories through Thalamus, in Engram's own format, with every stimulus checked against what you typed and keys, passwords and bank details refused. A tab Thalamus opens starts with its memory already open: one memory session per chat, kept through compacts and resumes, and a short briefing of its project's rulings and recent work, so the session spends no tokens or steps getting its memory back. The Rulings view lists a project's rulings in force, finds the stale ones on request, and retires or replaces them with one decision of yours.

A good pair
Drop an idea into Thalamus and it lands in the right Engram project. Start it from the Board, and the session picks up its project's memory. When it finishes, Thalamus tells you; Engram has already written down what happened.

Get Engram

Install

Up and running in two minutes.

  1. Download the installer

    Get Thalamus-Setup.exe from the latest release.

  2. Trust the signing certificate (once)

    Thalamus is signed with its own certificate, not one bought from a certificate authority. Windows has to be told to trust it once, otherwise the installer shows "Unknown publisher" and automatic updates are refused. Run this in PowerShell; it downloads the public certificate, checks its fingerprint and adds it for your user only (Windows asks you to confirm):

    $c = "$env:TEMP\thalamus-code-signing.cer"
Invoke-WebRequest https://martindroneharmony.github.io/thalamus-releases/thalamus-code-signing.cer -OutFile $c
if ((Get-PfxCertificate $c).Thumbprint -ne '3FD6F579455657834DBD444A55B9FC38F8CD8C49') { throw 'Fingerprint does not match, do not install.' }
Import-Certificate $c -CertStoreLocation Cert:\CurrentUser\Root | Out-Null
Import-Certificate $c -CertStoreLocation Cert:\CurrentUser\TrustedPublisher | Out-Null

    The certificate can only sign programs (nothing else, such as websites) and expires in October 2031. To remove it later, delete Thalamus Code Signing from both stores in certmgr.msc.

  3. Run it

    It installs for your user only, no admin rights needed. The publisher shows as Thalamus Code Signing. As a new program it may still meet "Windows protected your PC": choose More info, then Run anyway.

  4. Find it in the tray

    Thalamus starts with Windows and lives in the tray. Click the icon to open the cockpit; the gear (Ctrl+,) opens the settings.

  5. Let your sessions report

    Click Set up the hooks…. Thalamus shows exactly what it adds to your Claude settings and makes a backup first.

  6. Start Claude anywhere

    Run claude in any terminal, or press Ctrl+N in Thalamus and pick a project. The session appears in the Cockpit.

  7. Optional: your phone

    Install Tailscale on the laptop and the phone and switch on HTTPS certificates for your tailnet. In Settings › Phone, switch the phone page on and choose Pair a phone; scan the code with the phone's camera and set up Face ID. Add it to the Home Screen for notifications.

  8. Optional: limits and context fill

    If you use a Claude status line script in PowerShell, add this so it also passes the limits and each session's context fill to Thalamus ($raw is the JSON your script read from standard input):

    try { $t = Get-Content "$env:LOCALAPPDATA\Thalamus\report.json" -Raw -ErrorAction Stop | ConvertFrom-Json
      Invoke-RestMethod -Uri $t.url -Method Post -Headers @{ Authorization = "Bearer $($t.token)" } `
        -ContentType 'application/json' -Body $raw -TimeoutSec 1 | Out-Null } catch {}
